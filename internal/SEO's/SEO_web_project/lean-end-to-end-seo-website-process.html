Planning assumptions

The baseline timeline assumes:

  • 10–20 indexable launch pages
  • One language
  • One primary market
  • One SEO strategist and website consultant
  • One external copywriter
  • One client decision-maker
  • Claude Code used for design and development
  • Semrush used for keyword, competitor, ranking, backlink and technical research
  • Existing brand identity, or a light visual-direction exercise
  • No complex web application functionality
  • Client feedback within two working days
  • Copywriter works in parallel with design-system development

Expected duration

Project type Typical scope Duration
Small website5–8 pages, limited migration4–5 weeks
Standard service website10–20 pages6–8 weeks
Larger SEO website20–40 pages, several templates8–12 weeks
Complex migrationLarge existing site, redirect and content risks10–16 weeks

The standard process below is planned over seven weeks.

Phase 0 — Project setup

Timing: Day 1, approximately half a working day. Owner: SEO strategist and website consultant.

Objective: Create the project workspace and all intake instruments before asking Claude to analyse anything.

Human activities

  • Create the local repository or clone the existing project
  • Create the project folder structure
  • Create the client questionnaire
  • Create an access checklist
  • Schedule the kick-off interview
  • Ask the client for existing materials, brand files and evidence
  • Define the primary client approver
  • Agree feedback turnaround times

Phase 1 — Client intake and evidence collection

Timing: Days 1–3, starting immediately after project setup. Owner: SEO strategist and website consultant.

Objective: Collect the business facts, commercial priorities, existing evidence and access required for SEO research.

Client inputs

Business information: services and products · commercial priorities · profitability or strategic importance · target audiences · buying triggers · common objections · sales process · definition of a qualified lead · geographic markets · competitors · differentiators.

Existing materials: client-input/business-materials/ — service brochures, sales presentations, existing copy, company profile, pricing information.

Brand assets: client-input/brand-assets/ — logos, brand guidelines, imagery, iconography, colour and font references.

Evidence: client-input/proof/ — testimonials, client-logo permissions, certifications, case-study sources, documented results, claim register.

Access: Semrush · Google Search Console · Google Analytics · current CMS · existing source repository · CRM · hosting · domain and DNS · Tag Manager · Google Business Profile where relevant.

Client kick-off interview

A 60–90-minute interview, stored as kick-off-recording.m4a, kick-off-transcript.md, kick-off-notes.md.

Phase 2 — Semrush and first-party SEO research

Timing: Days 2–6, overlapping the latter part of client intake. Owner: SEO strategist and website consultant.

Objective: Produce the raw SEO evidence required for site architecture and page planning.

Tool responsibilities

Semrush — domain overview · existing organic keywords · existing ranking pages · organic competitors · keyword gaps and overlaps · keyword discovery · keyword metrics · SERP and intent research · backlink profile · competitor backlink comparison · position tracking · Site Audit · estimated competitor traffic where useful.

Search Console — first-party queries · pages · clicks · impressions · CTR · average position.

Analytics — organic landing pages · engagement · leads and conversions · conversion rates · revenue or lead quality where available.

Manual SERP review — actual search intent · page types ranking · local-pack presence · commercial versus informational results · directories, aggregators and comparison pages · important questions and SERP features.

Claude Code does not replace Semrush or manual SERP review. It analyses and consolidates their outputs.

2.1 Existing-domain analysis

Run Domain Overview · Organic Research · Organic Pages · Organic Competitors · Backlink Analytics · Site Audit · Position Tracking. Store as domain-overview.csv, organic-keywords.csv, organic-pages.csv, organic-competitors.csv, backlink-overview.csv, referring-domains.csv, site-audit.csv, position-tracking-baseline.csv.

Key questions: which pages already attract organic visibility · which URLs should be preserved · which queries already perform · which pages rank for multiple conflicting intents · who the real organic competitors are · where the strongest backlinks point · which technical issues affect migration or redesign.

2.2 Competitor discovery and comparison

Start with Semrush organic competitors rather than the client's known commercial competitors. Select ~3–5 direct organic competitors, 1–2 strong editorial or content competitors, and 1 aspirational competitor where relevant. Collect shared keywords · missing keywords · unique competitor keywords · ranking pages · estimated organic traffic · topic coverage · keyword overlap · backlink competitors. Store as competitor-domain-list.md, keyword-gap.csv, competitor-keywords.csv, competitor-pages.csv, backlink-gap.csv.

2.3 Seed-keyword research

Seed keywords come from client service names · customer language from interviews · Search Console queries · existing ranking keywords · competitor service pages · sales terminology · problem statements · geographic modifiers · relevant outcome terms.

Store seed-keywords.csv with columns: seed_keyword, source, service, audience, market, business_priority, notes.

For each seed or tightly related seed group, obtain search volume · keyword difficulty · CPC · competitive density · search intent · SERP features · related keywords · broad-match variations · phrase-match variations · questions · ranking URLs. Export into keyword-overview.csv, related-keywords.csv, broad-match-keywords.csv, keyword-questions.csv, serp-keyword-data.csv.

Do not export the entire possible keyword universe without filtering. Research stays focused on priority services · commercial problems · decision-stage questions · relevant supporting topics · genuine geographic markets.

2.4 Search Console and Analytics exports

queries-16-months.csv, pages-16-months.csv, query-page-combinations.csv; organic-landing-pages.csv, organic-conversions.csv, lead-quality-summary.csv. Use the longest useful comparable period, normally up to 16 months, separating recent performance from older data where the business or website has materially changed.

2.5 Existing-site crawl

Before any URL or migration decision: all-urls.csv, indexable-pages.csv, redirects.csv, canonicals.csv, metadata.csv, headings.csv, internal-links.csv, structured-data.csv, crawl-summary.md. The important point is that Claude receives structured exports rather than being asked to guess the existing site state.

2.6 Manual SERP review

After preliminary keyword data and before final intent clustering, the consultant manually reviews priority service keywords · ambiguous high-value terms · high-volume terms · keywords with mixed intent · location-related queries · keywords where Semrush intent classification appears questionable.

For each query record: keyword · country/device · dominant intent · secondary intent · dominant page type · local pack present · directories present · SERP features · leading competitors · implication for page strategy.

Phase 4 — Page strategies and copy briefs

Timing: Days 8–12, starting as soon as the architecture is stable. Owner: SEO strategist and website consultant.

Objective: Define the SEO, user and conversion role of each priority page. See Phase 6 of the canonical guide for the full prompt.

Copywriter handoff — by end of Week 2 the copywriter receives: page strategy · keyword and intent context · supporting terminology · search questions · proof register · required internal links · content schema or provisional section structure · length guidance.

Exit condition: priority page briefs approved and ready for copy production.

Phase 5 — Parallel work: copy architecture and design system

Timing: Week 3. Two workstreams run in parallel.

Workstream A — Copywriter message architecture

Owner: Copywriter. Duration: ~2–4 working days for the representative page and core page types.

content/drafts/
├── representative-service-page-message-architecture.md
├── homepage-message-architecture.md
└── service-overview-message-architecture.md

Claude reviews without rewriting, reporting only: intent misalignment · unanswered user questions · duplicated section functions · missing evidence · unsupported claims · weak CTA progression · missing internal links · likely layout pressure — each classified as strategy / copy / design or template / missing evidence.

Workstream B — Visual direction and shared design system

Owner: SEO strategist and website consultant. Production: Claude Code. Duration: ~3–5 working days.

Produces docs/design-direction.md, then tokens.css, reset.css, typography.css, layout.css, components.css, sections.css, forms.css, navigation.css, utilities.css, global.css and the system-preview page. The preview must show colours · typography · spacing · content and container widths · buttons · cards · forms · focus states · light and dark sections · mobile and desktop examples, and carries noindex metadata.

Week 3 approval gate: representative page message architecture · design direction · design tokens · shared CSS foundations · basic components.

Phase 6 — Representative service page

Timing: Week 4, ~4–5 working days. Owner: SEO strategist and website consultant. Production: Claude Code.

Objective: Validate design, structure, content model, SEO handling, responsive behaviour and component reuse on one page.

Build the minimum reusable system required: UI components · reusable sections · src/content-models/service-page.ts · src/templates/ServicePageTemplate.tsx · one development page with realistic structural placeholders. Support metadata · canonical · breadcrumbs · hero · problem · outcomes · service scope · process · proof · related services · FAQ · related content · final CTA · conversion tracking fields.

The consultant reviews at 375px mobile, tablet, standard laptop and wide desktop, checking hero hierarchy · section sequence · copy capacity · navigation · CTA visibility · internal-link placement · long-form readability · component reuse · responsive behaviour.

Approval gate — by end of Week 4 the representative page becomes the production standard.

Phase 7 — Final copy and template scaling

Timing: Week 5.

The copywriter finishes approved copy for the representative service page, homepage, service overview and other priority pages. Work started in Week 3, so Week 5 is completion, review and integration rather than starting from zero.

Claude validates the copy without editing it, integrates it preserving the wording exactly, and produces a mapping report. Template planning identifies which additional templates are genuinely required, preferring configuration, template variants and shared sections over duplicated template files.

Phase 9 — Pre-launch QA

Timing: Week 7, Days 1–3.

A page-level QA matrix covering route · render status · title · meta description · canonical · indexability · H1 · heading hierarchy · keyword-to-page alignment · internal links · broken links · redirects · image dimensions · alt text · structured data · mobile layout · keyboard access · forms · CRM submission · analytics events · conversion tracking · content completeness · template consistency · performance risks · build status — classified launch blocker / high / medium / low, with ownership assigned.

Human approvals: copywriter approves final wording and metadata wording · client approves factual accuracy, claims, testimonials, brand use and legal content · consultant approves SEO targeting, UX, design, responsive behaviour, accessibility, technical SEO, tracking and conversion flow.

Phase 10 — Launch and live validation

Timing: Week 7, Days 4–5.

Launch-readiness report

Check production build · environment variables · redirect rules · robots.txt · sitemap.xml · canonical host · staging noindex · production indexing · forms and CRM · analytics · consent · 404 behaviour · image optimisation · security headers · backup and rollback procedure. Separate ready / human action required / third-party access required / launch blockers / post-launch checks. Do not deploy automatically.

Deployment sequence

  1. Back up the current production state.
  2. Deploy the approved build.
  3. Connect the production domain.
  4. Apply redirect rules.
  5. Verify production environment variables.
  6. Confirm robots and sitemap.
  7. Test forms and CRM.
  8. Test analytics and conversion events.
  9. Crawl the live website.
  10. Submit or validate the sitemap in Search Console.

Then produce seo/post-launch-validation.md listing confirmed working · failures · probable causes · corrective actions.

After 6–8 weeksRanking, CTR, landing-page engagement, conversions
Monthly thereafterTechnical, ranking, content and conversion review

Semrush post-launch setup

Create or update the Semrush project with the correct production domain · target country and location · desktop and mobile tracking where relevant · approved priority keyword set · competitor domains · Site Audit crawl settings · Position Tracking campaign.

The tracked keyword set comes from the approved keyword-to-page map. It must not contain every keyword exported during research.

Monthly research sources

Semrush Position Tracking · Semrush Site Audit · Semrush Organic Research · Search Console · Analytics · CRM-qualified leads.

For each landing page, identify ranking problem · click-through problem · intent mismatch · conversion problem · cannibalisation · content gap · internal-link opportunity · technical issue · insufficient data. Prioritise qualified conversions, commercial importance and evidence strength. For every recommendation include evidence · hypothesis · proposed change · primary metric · guardrail metric · implementation scope · risk · evaluation condition. Do not recommend a full-page rewrite without sufficient evidence.

Critical path

The fastest possible project is not determined mainly by Claude Code's development speed. It is determined by four dependencies: client evidence and access · architecture approval · copywriter delivery · consolidated feedback.

Recommended seven-week schedule

Week 0 — Setup: create repository and folders · send questionnaire · request access and materials · schedule kick-off.

Week 1 — Discovery and research: conduct kick-off interview · gather evidence and brand assets · run Semrush domain and competitor research · run seed-keyword research · export Search Console and Analytics · crawl existing site · perform priority SERP review.

Week 2 — Architecture: cluster keywords · create keyword-to-page map · create sitemap and URL structure · create redirect plan · approve launch scope · produce priority page briefs · brief copywriter.

Week 3 — Copy structure and design system: copywriter develops message architectures · consultant approves design direction · Claude builds tokens, shared CSS and system preview · approve representative-page content structure.

Week 4 — Representative page: build service-page components and template · test responsive behaviour · review SEO structure and conversion flow · approve representative page.

Week 5 — Copy and templates: validate and integrate approved copy · resolve copy-fit issues · create required additional templates · begin producing remaining pages.

Week 6 — Full website: complete all launch pages · add forms, CRM, tracking and interactions · add internal links and structured data · complete staging review.

Week 7 — QA and launch: combined SEO, design, accessibility and technical QA · client and copywriter approval · redirect and tracking verification · deploy · run live validation.

Lean control rules

  1. Semrush research is conducted in Week 1, before keyword clustering and site architecture.
  2. Seed keywords originate from business discovery, Search Console, existing rankings, competitor pages and customer language.
  3. Semrush provides quantitative and competitive data; manual SERP review confirms actual intent.
  4. Claude Code processes structured exports but does not replace the SEO strategist's judgement.
  5. One representative page validates the system before all templates and pages are produced.
  6. The copywriter starts after page briefs are approved and works in parallel with design-system development.
  7. Feedback is consolidated into defined approval gates rather than collected continuously.
  8. The project launches with a deliberately limited page set; additional SEO pages are produced after launch according to the approved content roadmap.
