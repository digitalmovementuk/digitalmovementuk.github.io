1Week 1–2

Find out what people actually search for

Interview the client, pull the search data, then read the real search results by hand. Group the searches by what the person is trying to do, and turn those groups into a short list of pages. Nothing gets written or designed until this exists.

  • P1 Client interview
  • P2 Grouping the searches
  • P3 Page list & web addresses
Who
Lead
What comes out
Interview notes · a list of every claim and whether we can prove it · groups of searches · which search belongs on which page · the page list · a plan for the old web addresses
The rule
One page for each thing people want. Not one page per search term.

What we need from the client — meetings 1 and 2 · one written pack

  • Meeting 1 · Kick-off, logins and dates — 90 min, Week 1, recorded. The interview, the logins handed over live, and the other three meeting dates put in the diary before anyone leaves the call. One meeting instead of three.
  • Written pack A — sent the same day, back within 5 working days. Questions, logins, brochures, brand files, testimonials and permission to use them, websites they like, and the date their photos are due. One email, one list, one deadline — we never drip-feed requests.
  • Meeting 2 · Pages, claims and the real detail — 90 min, Week 2, the writer is there. Walk the page list and cut it down, go through every claim to see what we can prove, and talk to the person who actually does the work. All in one sitting, so the writer never has to book their own interviews.
Approval 1 The page list is agreed — which pages we build, what they are called, what happens to the old ones Client + lead

2Week 0 · 2

Set up the workspace

Create the project folders at the very start, and write the standing instructions the AI works to once the page list is settled. Half a day now, instead of weeks of mess later.

  • P4 Folders and files
  • P5 Standing instructions
Who
Lead AI
What comes out
The project folders · a short guide to what goes where · a running status file · the AI's standing instructions
The rule
Raw notes, drafts and approved work live in three separate folders. Only the approved folder goes into the site.

What we need from the client — nothing

Our own setup. Everything we needed arrived with pack A.

3Week 2–3

Decide what each page has to do

For every page: who it is for, which question it answers, what we want the reader to do next, and what we can prove. Then the writer plans the page section by section. This is where the writer takes over — and from here, writing and design run side by side.

  • P6 Page plan & writer's brief
  • P7 Section-by-section plan
Who
Lead → Writer
What comes out
A plan for each page · a brief the writer can work from · the section-by-section plan for the first page
The rule
No brief without proof. Anything we cannot back up stays off the page.

What we need from the client — nothing new

The claims and the real detail were taken in meeting 2, with the writer in the room. Anything still open goes to the one named approver in writing, all in one message, answered within two working days.

Approval 2 The page plans and the first section-by-section plan are agreed Lead, and the client on every claim

4Week 3

Build the look once, before any page

Agree the look, then build the colours, type, spacing, buttons, cards and forms as one set that every page shares. We prove it on a private sample page first. Nothing is styled page by page — that is what makes pages drift apart later.

  • P8 What the look should be
  • P9 Colours, type, spacing
  • P10 Private sample page
  • P11 Shared styling
  • P12 Reusable parts
  • P13 Reusable sections
  • P14 What content each page holds
Who
Lead decides AI builds
What comes out
The agreed look, written down · one shared set of styles · a kit of buttons, cards and forms · a list of the sections a page can be built from · what content every page needs
The rule
Anything that appears on more than one page is built once and shared.

What we need from the client — nothing

The look comes from the websites they liked and the brand files in pack A. The sample page is for us, not for them — a page of buttons tells a client nothing. They see the look working on a real page at meeting 3.

Approval 3 The look is agreed on the sample page — colours, type, spacing, buttons, forms Lead

5Week 4

Build one real page and test everything on it

One real service page, built from the shared set, checked on a phone first and then on a large screen. It shows whether the layout holds real writing, whether the page works for search, and whether the parts fit together — before we build twenty more the same way.

  • P15 The first real page
Who
AI builds Lead checks
What comes out
One finished page, and the pattern every other page is copied from
The rule
This is the approval that matters most. Everything after it is repetition.

What we need from the client — meeting 3 · the one that matters

  • Meeting 3 · First page review — 60 min, Week 4, shown on a phone first. The look, the layout and the page structure are all agreed here, on something the client can actually judge. Last ten minutes: where enquiries should go, who gets the email, and any cookie or consent rules.
  • Comments in writing within two working days — no meeting. One document, one voice, from the one named approver. Two rounds of changes are included; a third is a change request with a price on it.
Approval 4 The first page is agreed, and becomes the pattern for the rest Lead + client

6Week 5

The final writing goes in, word for word

The writer finishes. We check the writing against the brief and the list of provable claims without changing a word, put it in exactly as written, then flag anywhere the words and the layout fight each other so a person can decide which one gives.

  • P16 Writer finishes
  • P17 Checking it
  • P18 Putting it in
  • P19 Words against layout
Who
Writer AI
What comes out
The approved writing in one folder · a note of where every piece landed · a list of anywhere the words and the layout clash
The rule
The AI never edits approved writing. It reports the problem; a person decides.

What we need from the client — one written pack · no meeting unless it is needed

  • Written pack B · the writing, for approval — back within 3 working days. All the writing in one document, with the claim decisions already applied. The client checks facts, names, numbers and anything a regulator would read twice, then approves it in the same document.
  • Legal or compliance checks happen inside pack B, not after it. Where the industry requires it, allow 2–10 working days and start the day the writing settles — never the week before launch.
  • A call only if the comments contradict each other — 30 min. Booked only if we need it. If the approver speaks with one voice, this meeting never happens.
Approval 5 The writing is approved — wording, page titles, claims, and how it fits Writer + client

▼

7Week 5–6

Build the rest of the pages

The remaining page types, every page, the forms and where enquiries go, the tracking, and the technical settings search engines read. Repetitive on purpose: if this stage needs new thinking, something earlier was agreed too soon.

  • P20 Other page types
  • P21 Every page built
  • P22 Forms & tracking
  • P23 Technical search settings
Who
AI Lead checks
What comes out
The whole site on a test address · page titles and descriptions · the settings search engines read · tracking that works
The rule
Reuse what exists. Build a new page type only when nothing can be adapted.

What we need from the client — one deadline, no meeting

  • Final photos and files — the deadline was agreed back in meeting 1. Photography, logos in usable formats, team names and photos, any certification marks. Missing photos are the most common reason Week 6 slips — which is why we set the date in Week 1 and do not ask for them in Week 6.
  • A short written update each week — five minutes to read, no reply needed. This replaces the progress call, and it is what stops "we haven't seen anything for two weeks".

▼

8Week 7

Check it, approve it, put it live, then prove it works

Every page goes through the same checklist, with each problem written down and given an owner. Three people approve separately. Then we put it live and test the live site the same day. The AI never puts anything live on its own.

  • P24 The full check
  • P25 Getting ready to go live
  • P26 Testing it live
Who
All three
What comes out
The page-by-page check · the go-live checklist · the live site · a report on what we tested after launch
The rule
Nothing goes live with a serious problem still open. A backup and a way back exist before we switch.

What we need from the client — meeting 4 · then in writing

  • Meeting 4 · Walkthrough and launch date — 60 min, Week 7. The client clicks through the test site with us, sends a real enquiry, and sees the real email arrive. Then we agree the launch date and who is reachable on the day. Both in one sitting.
  • Written approval to go live — within 2 working days. Facts, claims, testimonials, use of the brand, legal pages. In writing, never verbal — this is the record if a claim is ever challenged.
  • A written handover after launch — no meeting. What changed, what to watch, where the numbers live, what happens next.
Approval 6 Cleared to go live — search and technical, writing and page titles, facts and legal Lead + writer + client

9Ongoing

Watch it, then improve it every month

Rankings, clicks, which pages people land on, and how many enquiries came in — all looked at together, once a month. Every suggested change comes with the evidence behind it, what we expect to happen, and the date we will check. What we learn goes back into Stage 1.

  • P27 Monthly review and improvements
Who
Lead
What comes out
A one-page monthly review · a short list of what to change next · the plan for the next set of pages
The rule
No rewriting a page on a hunch. We track the searches we chose, not everything we researched.

What we need from the client — one meeting a month

  • Monthly review — 30 min. One page, three decisions, no tour of the dashboard. Every third month the same slot runs 30 minutes longer and covers the plan for the next set of pages — nothing extra in the diary.
  • Which enquiries were any good — two minutes a month, in writing. The client marks which enquiries were worth having. Without that we end up chasing form fills instead of revenue.

What actually sets the timeline

What holds everything up

  1. Client answers and logins — no research without them
  2. Agreeing the page list — nothing after it can start
  3. The writer finishing — usually the longest single step
  4. Feedback in one voice — two working days, or the schedule slips day for day

How fast we build is not the constraint. These four are.

How much client time this takes

  • 4 meetings before launch — Weeks 1, 2, 4 and 7
  • 5 hours of client time in meetings, in total
  • 2 written packs — everything else is written, not scheduled
  • Turnaround: 2 working days for comments, 3 for the writing
  • Two rounds of changes per approval; a third is priced
  • After launch: 30 minutes a month

All four dates go in the diary during meeting 1. A meeting booked late is the delay — and a meeting that could have been a document is a meeting we do not hold.

What makes it take longer

  • No usable brand look to start from — 3–5 days
  • More than 20 pages — 1–3 weeks
  • A second language — 1–3 weeks
  • Moving a big old website across — 1–3 weeks
  • Legal review — 2–10 days
  • Lots of claims we cannot prove yet — depends on the client

When to stop and go back

  • Building pages before the page list is agreed
  • Styling a page before the shared look is agreed
  • Copying the pattern before the first real page is approved
  • Writing going into the build before the client has checked the claims
  • The AI editing writing that has been approved
  • The AI putting anything live without a person
  • A third round of changes without a change request and a price

How we brief the AI

  • What to read — the exact files
  • What to produce — the exact output
  • What not to do — the limits
  • How to check itself — before handing anything back
  • When to stop and ask — what it must never decide alone

Every instruction in the full guide has all five. The AI builds; a person decides.

Seven-week timeline

A standard project: 10–20 pages, one language, one person approving. A small site runs 4–5 weeks; 20–40 pages runs 8–12; moving a large old website across runs 10–16.
